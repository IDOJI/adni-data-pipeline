# Resting-state fMRI studies using fMRIPrep and XCP-D

This reading list covers nine publications that use the two tools on resting-state data, including studies that apply them only to a replication dataset or alongside task fMRI. The comparison uses each paper's stated workflow and the repository's published 7.B → 10 defaults. **NR** means that the cited source does not specify a setting.

## Reading a functional-to-anatomical alignment panel

Open the original fMRIPrep HTML and toggle between the BOLD reference and T1w. Follow the ventricles and corpus callosum across several slices and all three planes. A consistent offset between the intensity boundaries across views warrants review. Dark or compressed BOLD regions can also reflect signal loss or susceptibility distortion. In a single static frame the red contours alone cannot establish whether the two source images align.

Provins et al. describe this exact panel in §2.4.3.6 and give exclusion criteria in Table 3. Their Figure 2B–C shows frontal signal loss that is conspicuous in one plane and subtle in another. Supplementary Figure 11 illustrates residual distortion. Their decisions reflect a whole-brain voxel-wise study. [Paper, figures and supplement](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnimg.2022.1073734).

## Paper-by-paper methods

FD is framewise displacement. A run-level FD exclusion or interpolation of a high-motion frame has a different effect from omitting that frame in the final time series. Filter frequencies are in Hz, and smoothing widths are FWHM.

## How each paper compares with 7.B → 10

| Study | Shared choices | Differences that matter for reproducing the analysis |
|---|---|---|
| Luo | 36 terms including GSR, FD calculated using a 50 mm radius, despiking, MNI152NLin6Asym and generation of 91k CIFTI. | Different versions across cohorts, rest and task concatenation in the primary analysis, Schaefer 200 for the primary FC, and cohort-specific distortion correction including reverse-phase encoding acquisitions. |
| Li | 36 terms including GSR, removal of high-motion output frames and 6 mm smoothing. | Acquired reverse-phase field-map inputs, six initial volumes removed in preprocessing, 0.5 mm rather than 0.3 mm FD, 0.008 Hz lower cutoff, and seed-to-site FC. |
| Kim | 36P with GSR, despiking, 0.01–0.08 Hz filtering and 6 mm smoothing in MICA-MICs. | Schaefer 400 and a timescale endpoint. The 0.5 mm fMRIPrep outlier threshold is an annotation. The primary HCP-YA data followed a separate workflow. |
| Byeon | 36 terms including GSR and the 0.01–0.08 Hz passband in NKI-RS. | NKI-RS grayordinate workflow uses Schaefer 200 and propagation analysis. The HCP cohorts' processing settings do not describe the NKI-RS workflow. |
| Wu | 36 nuisance terms, 0.01–0.08 Hz, 6 mm smoothing and ALFF. | Different versions, ten initial volumes discarded and FD >0.5 mm with a 66.70 mm radius. Brainnetome 246 regional ALFF is used for NMF. |
| Zeng | 2 mm MNI152NLin6Asym, 36 terms including GSR, despiking, 0.01–0.08 Hz filtering and 6 mm smoothing. | Ten initial volumes discarded and interpolation based on filtered FD >0.5 mm. Power-based sliding-window analysis instead of one FC matrix per run. |
| Abdolalizadeh | MNI152NLin6Asym, GSR and recensoring of high-motion frames. | Tissue-component model, FD >0.5 mm, wider band-pass, unsmoothed output, and different cortical and subcortical atlases. |
| Gao | 36P with GSR, despiking, 0.3 mm frame censoring, 0.01–0.08 Hz and 6 mm. | Uses CIFTI for denoising, omits susceptibility distortion correction and uses a 0.25 mm mean-FD participant exclusion. Its endpoint is Schaefer 400 cortical gradients. |
| Huang | fMRIPrep derivatives, a 36P/GSR variant, 4S256 among the atlases, ALFF and ReHo. | Also releases 32-term and high-pass variants. The reported band-pass is wider, the volume workflow uses MNI152NLin2009cAsym, and reported FC uses unsmoothed data. |
